# Settings style

The rules for Needle's Settings pages on every system (Windows, Linux, macOS, iPhone,
Android). Each system draws them in its own way (Windows 11 cards, macOS grouped forms, iOS
inset lists, Material 3 groups), but the rules are the same. Read this before adding or
changing a setting.

## A setting

- One row: a small icon, a short name, **one** short line, and the control on the right.
- The line says what the setting does, in plain words. No second line. Longer text goes
  inside a row that opens, into a tooltip, or away.
- Sentence case. No "Enable" or "Allow" in front of the name.
- The control is the system's own (a switch, a segmented choice, a slider, a button).

## Groups

- Settings sit in small groups under one short heading (for example "Playing", "Layout").
- No grey notes under a group that only repeat what its rows say.
- A page's first group sits right under the page title.

## Pictures

- Pictures only where the choice is about how Needle looks: the look (themes) and the
  background. They are picture cards with a thin accent line and a check on the chosen one.
- A choice about where things sit (the player, song rows, the search field) is a row with
  two small grey sketches, where only the changing part is in the accent.
- Everything else is a plain row. No big tiles, no glows, no preview box.

## Colour

- The accent marks only what is chosen, or the one main action of a form (Connect, Sign in).
- Boxes are flat, with a thin line or the system's own grouped background, not filled panels.

## Rows that open

- Details that most people never change open under their row, with an arrow. Closed, the
  row shows the current choice on the right (for example "System default", or "Off").
- Sign-in forms stay closed until asked: a row with the status ("Not connected", "Not
  signed in") and a Connect or Sign in… button. The form opens under it, with Cancel.
- When nothing needs filling in (Needle's own Last.fm key is built in), Connect starts the
  sign-in at once.

## Adding a setting

1. First ask whether it needs to be a setting at all. If there is no reason to pick the
   other choice, do the right thing without asking (for example, keep one copy of a file).
2. If it belongs to another setting, it is a **dependent row**: shown under that setting,
   indented or in the same group, and only while that setting is on. For example, "On
   mobile data" under "Moving covers" on the phones.
3. Never a new group or page for one switch. Put it in the group it belongs to.
4. Only on the systems where it means something (no mobile-data setting on computers).
5. One short name, one short line, a default that suits most people.
6. The same setting has the same name and line on every system.
